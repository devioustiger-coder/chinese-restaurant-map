# Bellevue, Seattle, and San Jose case-study framework

Updated November 22, 2026

## Design

Keep the 30-city association between Chinese population share and sampled Chinese-eatery availability as the baseline. Use the three focus cities to ask why a city can depart from that baseline and whether restaurant availability translates into equitable access and cultural belonging.

## Current descriptive findings

| City | Chinese population share | Sampled Chinese eateries | Sampled eateries per 100,000 | Completeness signal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bellevue | 16.4% | 51 | 33.59 | Yelp/Google pilot found omissions plus Taiwanese taxonomy exclusions |
| Seattle | 5.8% | 97 | 12.86 | Yelp pilot matched well; Google still surfaced missing businesses |
| San Jose | 8.7% | 102 | 10.30 | Strongest undercoverage in the pilot; several missing businesses confirmed in county records |

These are observations within the OSM-derived sample, not complete city restaurant counts.

## Case-study questions and measurements

| Dimension | Core question | Comparable indicators | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial conditions | Do rents, shopping centers/corridors, permitting, and business turnover help explain differences in restaurant availability? | Retail rent, vacancy, commercial land area, permits, openings/closures, business age, mall/corridor location | City economic-development reports, parcel/assessor data, permit and food-facility records |
| Mobility | Which populations can reach Chinese restaurants within reasonable travel time, regardless of where they live? | 15/30/45-minute transit and driving catchments, stop frequency, parking, jobs/daytime population, customer origins | GTFS, street networks, parking data, LEHD/LODES, optional customer survey/interviews |
| Inequality | Do communities have equal affordable and accessible access to these spaces? | Income, car ownership, disability access, language, price level, displacement, business survival, geographic gaps | ACS, accessibility audits, menu prices, rent/closure history, community testimony |
| Cultural belonging | How do restaurants support identity, gathering, language, and community life, and whose experiences are missing? | Interview themes, community events, languages/services, perceptions of welcome/loss | Owner/customer interviews, observation, community organizations, carefully coded review text |

## City-specific hypotheses to investigate

### Bellevue

- The high sampled per-capita rate may be associated with a large Chinese population share, higher regional purchasing power, and commercial clusters, but the current analysis cannot identify the mechanism.
- The city retail study makes affordability, access to capital, tenant improvements, landlord negotiation, and displacement testable commercial questions.
- Mobility analysis should include trips to downtown and BelRed from homes and workplaces rather than treating restaurant tracts as self-contained markets.

### Seattle

- The city has more sampled restaurants in absolute terms but a lower per-capita rate than Bellevue.
- The Chinatown–International District should be studied as a regional destination and cultural district, not only as the neighborhood of nearby residents.
- Historical underinvestment, displacement, safety, and legacy-business preservation connect restaurant geography to inequality and cultural belonging.

### San Jose

- The city has the largest sampled count of the three but the lowest sampled per-capita rate.
- County validation indicates that OSM undercoverage is an especially important measurement problem before interpreting this difference.
- Malls, commercial corridors, immigrant histories, and travel across a large city are plausible case-study dimensions, not yet proven explanations.

## Evidence rules

1. Label correlations, counts, and validation rates as original project analysis.
2. Label city reports, news, and prior research as background evidence.
3. Do not call cuisine-tagged restaurants Chinese-owned businesses.
4. Do not interpret Yelp/Google ratings as neutral measures of quality or belonging.
5. Do not infer customers' home locations from restaurant census tracts.
6. Treat interviews and community partnerships as necessary evidence for belonging and missing voices.
